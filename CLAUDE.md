## 1. The project
A delivery marketplace for parcels and goods (NOT people) inside Algiers, scalable later to all of Algeria. It looks and works like inDrive, adapted to parcels (owner's wish, Oct 2026, with his reference pack of screenshots: dark map-first screens, bottom panels, side menu, lime main action, request-and-offer prices). Our own name, logo, icons and colour shade; never copy private technology.
Built so far by Claude in a claude.ai chat (October 2026), then moved to Claude Code. The original research documents stay in the owner's claude.ai project "delivery app"; they are background only, and this file overrides them.

## 4. Product decisions (current)
- **Coverage (owner's decision, Oct 2026): the whole of Algeria as a country is open from the start** (migration 021 creates the open zone "Algérie" = the country's border + 5 km, so coastal and border addresses are never cut off; places abroad are refused). Staff can still close it or add/close smaller zones (new console zones start closed). **Every delivery is local: the longest trip allowed is 50 km** (set with the prices, `max_km`; quote refuses `TOO_FAR`). Why 50 km: it covers every trip inside the wilaya of Algiers (Zéralda to Rouïba is about 50 km by road) and the nearest suburbs, while keeping parcels on short same-hour motorbike trips; longer trips are inter-wilaya transport, which is excluded.
- **Immediate orders only** (no scheduling).
- **One app, two paths.** The first screen asks "I want to send parcels" (User) or "I want to deliver" (Driver). No language step (owner's wish, Oct 2026): the app starts in the phone's language (Arabic, French, else English); it is changed only in Settings.
- **Login: Google sign-in for now** (free; owner's wish, Oct 2026). Phone + SMS-code login stays built but is OFF (`LOGIN_SMS`) until an SMS provider is chosen (each SMS costs money). The server tells the app which buttons to show (`login_methods`). A Google account must type a contact phone number (drivers and senders call each other); it is NOT verified, so it is not unique and is never used to find a person. iPhone publishing will also need "Sign in with Apple" (Apple's rule when Google login is offered).
- **One Google account (or one verified phone number) = one account with up to two INDEPENDENT profiles** (User, Driver). At login only the chosen path's profile is created; the second is added later only from Settings. Switching needs no new SMS. Each profile has its own data, ratings, status and notifications. Tokens are bound to one profile.
- **A driver profile never gets an order created by the same account** (stops fake deliveries, ratings and cash tricks).
- **Customers:** individuals and online sellers, equal focus, same app (no separate seller website).
- **Price (inDrive style, owner's wish Oct 2026):** the system recommends a price (base + per km + size/car extras, rounded up to 10 DA). The customer may set any price inside a staff-set range (min %/max % of recommended) and may RAISE it while searching. Each courier who sees the request accepts the customer's price or proposes a higher one (never above the maximum); the customer chooses among the offers by price, time to arrive, rating and deliveries, or switches on auto-accept (first courier who accepts his price). The price LOCKS when the customer chooses. Why: renegotiation after acceptance is inDrive's top complaint, so there is none after the choice. Price rules are versioned; each order keeps its version.
- **Vehicle:** decided by parcel size (small/medium → motorbike, large → car). The user never picks. A car courier also sees small and medium parcels (a car carries anything); a motorbike never sees large ones.
- **Couriers on the map (owner's wish, Oct 2026):** the customer's home map and the searching order show online couriers around the pin as small cars/motorbikes (`nearby_couriers`: positions rounded to ~100 m, no name or id), and as soon as the search starts the map zooms in exactly onto the pickup (0.9 s), then zooms out clearly and fast by itself (zoom 17 → 13.5, ~6 km across, in 30 s; the camera is placed directly every 50 ms instead of one queued native animation, which never moved on the owner's phone; starts once the map has loaded), stays centred on the pickup, cannot be moved by hand, and shows an inDrive-like pulse: a faint blue disc that starts small and grows in step with the zoom-out to a limited size (same start, same 30 s, both linear), with waves leaving the centre again and again (`ui/Pulse`, screen-sized; AppMap `follow` drives the camera and locks gestures). It only pictures "searching" and is deliberately NOT tied to the real search area (owner's wish). A courier who is online and free sees the same pulse around himself.
- **Matching:** a searching request is shown to up to 10 eligible couriers at once, nearest first, inside a circle that grows smoothly: 1 km at the start, +1 km every 8 s, up to 8 km (reached after 56 s); why: the nearest couriers get it first (shortest pickup) and farther ones join within a minute (one formula `searchRadiusM`, settings SEARCH_START_M/STEP_M/STEP_S/MAX_M); a courier's offer waits 40 s for the customer; one pending offer per courier; **a request never ends by itself** (owner's decision, Oct 2026: it searches until a courier takes it or the customer cancels; `SEARCH_WINDOW_S` = 0 by default, a request stays in a courier's list for a day). Starting values (`backend/src/common/config.ts`), to be tuned with real data. A courier holds one job at a time.
- **No receiver confirmation (owner's decision, Oct 2026, replacing the earlier "receiver says yes first"):** couriers are searched as soon as the order is created. The receiver may press "I am expecting it" on the link (optional, shown to sender and driver) or report a problem.
- **Trust and safety (owner's wish, Oct 2026):** every handover is proved by two people (pickup code + parcel photo from sender to driver; delivery code or QR from receiver to driver), positions are recorded, everything is in a history nobody can edit, rules with points give automatic TEMPORARY blocks, permanent bans only by staff, one appeal per penalty, banned accounts cannot come back (Google account, device, driver ID number). Full rules and the research behind them: `backend/docs/TRUST_AND_SAFETY.md`.
- **Receiver tracking page:** the sender shares a secret link (one tap opens a WhatsApp chat with the receiver, message ready; or any other app); without an account the receiver sees the map, time left, amount to pay, the code and QR. Why: GrabExpress/GoSend/Uber do this; receivers are mostly not app users.
- **Delivery proof:** a 4-digit delivery code. The sender sees it in the app and gives it to the recipient; a recipient whose VERIFIED number belongs to a registered user also gets it by push (typed, unverified numbers never do). After 5 wrong codes the order locks; staff can unlock it. Staff can never see the code.
- **Cash on delivery:** a permanent cash ledger per driver (never edited; corrections are new entries; a reversal works once). It records only the goods money (COD). Staff record handovers with numbered receipts. Each driver has a cash limit set at approval (0 = no COD orders); drivers over their limit get no COD offers.
- **Failed delivery** (no answer / wrong address / refused: damaged, wrong item, not ordered, changed mind, cannot pay): a return leg back to the sender starts automatically, plus a problem report for staff. Cancelling is allowed only before pickup (late sender cancels and drivers giving a job back count as rule points); after pickup the sender may call the parcel back.
- **Ratings:** 1–5 stars plus an optional comment, both directions, once per order. **Public profiles (owner's wish, Oct 2026):** the customer opens the courier's profile from his offer or the courier card, the courier opens the sender's from a request or his job: name, vehicle, star average, deliveries done / parcels sent, member since, and the latest 20 comments shown WITHOUT who wrote them (`public_profile`, reachable only through an offer or order the viewer is part of; no profile ids, so nobody can browse people).
- **Notifications:** push only (live WebSocket + phone push). Atomic: written in the same database transaction as the event. Missed ones replay on reconnect, never twice, one profile only. **No SMS except the login verification code (and that is off for now). No email** at launch.
- **Languages:** Arabic (right-to-left), French, English everywhere, including push texts (wording must be reviewed by native speakers).
- **No mock data**, anywhere. Every list has an empty state.
- **Excluded:** food, groceries, warehousing, inter-wilaya network, international, dangerous or high-value goods.
- **Account deletion (Google Play / Apple rule):** Menu → Settings → Delete my account (refused while an order, a job or senders' cash is still open); identity and document photos are erased, records that protect others stay anonymised. Public pages `/privacy` and `/delete-account` on the server (3 languages; wording is a draft until a lawyer reviews it, section 8).
- **Removed to keep it simple** (the owner may bring any back): scheduled delivery, seller website / CSV import / labels / seller API, saved addresses on the server (the last 6 destinations are kept on the phone only, for one-tap "recent"), in-app chat, choosing a vehicle, email.

## 5. Technical decisions
- One well-organised backend (a "modular monolith"), not microservices; no Kubernetes. Redis is NOT used yet (rate limits are in memory, live events use PostgreSQL LISTEN/NOTIFY); add it only when running several servers.
- Money is whole dinars (integers). Rules that protect money live in the database itself (price lock, one job per driver, append-only ledger and history).
- **Maps: free, open, no key, no payment card** (decided). Why: Google Maps needs a billing account with a card; open tools give the same features for a delivery app at no cost and with no lock-in.
  - *Map display in the app:* MapLibre React Native (open source) with vector tiles from OpenFreeMap's public service, using OUR style `backend/public/map/style.json` served at `/map/style.json` (OpenFreeMap "liberty" recoloured by `backend/deploy/map-style.py` like inDrive's night map: dark grey land, dark green parks, ochre main roads, blue-grey streets, light labels; owner's wish Oct 2026, the plain dark style was too black and the light one too white; changing the file restyles every map with no app update; free, no key, no registration, no stated view limits; OpenStreetMap data). The tile style address is a setting, never hard-coded, so we can switch to self-hosted tiles if needed. The OpenStreetMap Foundation's own tile server is NOT used (its policy forbids heavy app use). Credit "© OpenStreetMap contributors" must be shown on the map. MapLibre needs an Expo development build (not Expo Go); Expo's free cloud build covers that.
  - *Road distance and time:* our own OSRM server (open source) on the API server, using OpenStreetMap roads for the Algiers area (`backend/deploy/osrm.md`). All routing goes through ONE class (`backend/src/geo/routing.ts`, setting `ROUTING_URL`). If OSRM is off, slow (>1.5 s) or finds no road, the quote falls back to the straight-line estimate ×1.3 at 20 km/h and says `distance_estimated: true`. The public OSRM demo server is not used (non-commercial only).
  - *Addresses:* the person moves the map under a fixed centre pin (or uses "my location"), or searches a place, and types street, building and a landmark (landmarks matter more than street numbers in Algiers). Search and the street name under the pin go through OUR server (`backend/src/geo/places.ts`, setting `GEOCODER_URL`, Photon API = free open-source search over OpenStreetMap). The test server uses komoot's public Photon (fair use only, not for launch); a real launch runs its own Photon server. Empty setting = search off, the app hides the box. The OpenStreetMap Foundation's Nominatim is not used (its policy forbids app-scale use).
  - *Verified:* OpenFreeMap's style and Algiers tiles load (checked 2026-10-19: the city-centre tile has real streets and places). *Not yet verified:* OSRM on real Algiers roads, because this cloud machine's network still blocks `download.geofabrik.de` (OpenStreetMap data); OSRM is tested on a made-up test grid only.
- Push: Firebase Cloud Messaging (free) for Android and iPhone; wired on the server and in the app (the app registers the phone's token for the active profile and language), never tested with a real Firebase key. While the app is open, push banners are silenced (the live connection already shows the event). Builds: Expo's free cloud build (`mobile/eas.json`, `mobile/BUILD.md`). **App changes reach the phone without reinstalling** (decided Oct 2026, owner's wish): EAS Update (over-the-air updates, free up to 1,000 phones a month) is added in the next APK; after that, screen and text changes are sent with `eas update` and arrive when the app is reopened. A new APK is still needed only for native changes (new native libraries, app settings, Firebase file). **Live work (owner's wish, Oct 2026):** the owner's Windows laptop runs the code (Claude desktop app, Code tab, `npx expo start`) and his Android phone runs the development version (EAS profile `development`, `expo-dev-client`), which shows every edit within seconds over Wi-Fi; steps in `mobile/LIVE_DEV.md`. Update code signing stays off (see SECURITY.md, node-forge); Android package `dz.delivery.app` is a placeholder that must be final before the first Google Play upload.
- SMS provider: not chosen (stub in `backend/src/auth/sms.service.ts`); SMS login is off. Each login code costs money; get a real price quote before choosing.
- **Free test server (decided Oct 2026, owner's choice): Render (server, free, no card) + Neon (database, free, no card, PostGIS)**, Frankfurt. `render.yaml` + `backend/deploy/render-start.sh`; owner's steps in `backend/deploy/render.md`. The same server hands out the staff console at `/staff/` (one address, nothing cross-site to configure). Limits: sleeps after 15 min idle (about 1 min to wake), ~1 GB database, no OSRM (straight-line estimate). For testing only; a real server comes before launch. Oracle's free machine was rejected (needs a card, sign-up often fails).
- Google sign-in in the app: `@react-native-google-signin/google-signin` (free version), Firebase mode: enabled only when `mobile/google-services.json` exists (`app.config.js`); web client id in `EXPO_PUBLIC_GOOGLE_WEB_CLIENT_ID`. The server checks Google's proof against `GOOGLE_CLIENT_IDS`. The app's signing fingerprint (Expo keystore) SHA-1 `FB:15:52:C2:E6:1F:2E:F5:37:85:F1:5D:63:B5:BF:C7:25:FE:77:B2` must be registered in Firebase (and Google Play's own one later).
- Mobile: React Native + Expo, chosen over Flutter (Flutter could not be built or tested in the original environment; Expo's free cloud builds iPhone apps without a Mac). Follow `mobile/AGENTS.md` for Expo APIs.
- **Cloud work setup:** the project runs in a Claude Code cloud session (works with the owner's laptop off). `.claude/hooks/session-start.sh` prepares any fresh machine (PostGIS, PostgreSQL, packages, test database). A scheduled task "Resume delivery-app work" wakes the session every 2 hours to continue from `PROGRESS.md` (so work restarts after usage limits reset). Limit: it only wakes THIS session; if the session is archived or deleted, the owner starts a new session on this repo and says "continue from PROGRESS.md".
- **Mobile structure:** Expo Router (screens in `mobile/src/app`), own small translation module (`src/i18n`, no extra library). Bottom panels (`ui/Sheet`, always floating over a full-screen map; a centre pin stays visible because panels open at most 42% on pin screens) and the side menu (`ui/Drawer`) are plain React Native (no gesture/animation native library), and icons are `@expo/vector-icons` Feather (JavaScript + font), so the whole redesign reaches phones as an over-the-air update. New native libraries only when truly needed (they need a new APK). Direction (right-to-left for Arabic) is applied by the app itself on every screen, and the phone's own RTL switching is turned off (`supportsRTL: false`), so changing language works instantly without restarting the app. Only the 3 Cairo weights used are bundled. `react-native-web` is a test tool only (screenshots for design checks), not a product.
- **Driver document photos:** uploaded one by one (`upload_document`, raw image body, max 6 MB), decoded and redrawn by the server (sharp) so hidden data is dropped, stored in the private database (table `document_files`, never in a web folder). Why the database (changed Oct 2026): free hosts have no permanent disk, and one place to protect and back up is simpler. Staff see them only through `get_document` with their login, every view audited; no shareable links (safer than the expiring links first planned: nothing to leak in logs or chats). Unused photos are deleted after a day. The phone shrinks photos to 2000 px before sending (less mobile data). **Driver sign-up (owner's list, Oct 2026):** first and last name, date of birth (18–80), ID card number; vehicle type, colour, plate number; photos of the ID card, driving licence, proof that the vehicle is his, and 1–4 vehicle photos (plate readable on one). Photos are NEVER refused automatically for quality, size or how the phone labels the file (only a file that is not a picture at all is refused): staff approve or reject every driver by hand. The app sends the picture's JPEG bytes directly (reading the file back lost photos on some Android phones).
- **Customer flow (UX, inDrive-style):** home = full-screen map with the pickup under the centre pin, a pickup card at the top (street name), the side-menu button, and a bottom panel "Where to, and for how much?" with recent destinations; then the destination (search or map); then one screen with the route on the map and a panel: size cards, the fare with round −/+ buttons (10 DA), auto-accept, receiver, addresses, payment. The order screen keeps the map (A, B, route, courier) with a panel per state: searching (no countdown, owner's wish; couriers told, raise with −/+ then confirm, auto-accept), couriers' offer cards on top (price + minutes first, Decline/Accept), then courier card with time left, codes, history. It follows the order live and checks every 4 s while active. One idempotency key per order screen. If the price expired, it is recalculated and the person confirms again.
- **Courier work (UX):** map with my position, an online pill at the top, today's numbers; while online the phone sends its position (foreground only: every 15 s, plus the last reading again every 30 s while standing still, because the server ignores positions older than 2 minutes; the first reading never waits more than 10 s; a warning shows while no position is known; the app stays open while working, no background location for now: simpler, and Google Play's background-location review is avoided). Requests near me arrive live in the panel (5 s check as fallback): price, distances, cash to collect (amber + icon); opened: size, sender rating, "Accept for X DA" or "offer more" (+50/+100/+200), "not for me"; then "waiting for the customer" with a countdown; chosen = the job opens by itself. The job screen (map on top, panel below) gives one action at a time; calls and turn-by-turn navigation use the phone's own dialer and map app (free). Cash to collect must be confirmed before "delivered". The driver rates the sender after a delivery (returns are not rated). Without network, "picked up" and "delivered" are saved on the phone and sent automatically later (the driver can continue); a delivery code is then checked by the server only when it arrives, and a refusal is shown to the driver.
- **Working app name** until the owner chooses: "Delivery" / "Livraison" / "توصيل".
- Publishing: Android first (Google Play is $25 one-time, or a direct APK). iPhone is built but published later (Apple $99/year).

## 6. Security (must stay true; full list in `backend/docs/SECURITY.md`)
- Free tools only. Honest goal: many layers, a trace for every attempt, no single secret opens everything. Nothing is "unhackable".
- The app connects to the database as the LIMITED role `delivery_app` (cannot edit history, ledger, audit, staff accounts or the schema).
- Staff login: password + authenticator code (2FA), lockout, 2-hour tokens, permanent audit trail.
- Rotating refresh tokens with theft detection; 15-min access tokens; max 5 devices; new-login alerts; "log out everywhere".
- Rate limits, SMS-fraud caps, strict input checks, security headers, a permanent security log.
- Test hooks exist only with `TEST_HOOKS=1`; a production server refuses to start with it. Tests refuse to run on a database named `delivery`.
- Mobile keeps the login key in the phone's secure vault and sends exactly ONE token renewal at a time.
- Add a new attack test whenever you add an endpoint. Run `npm audit --omit=dev` in each project before releases.

## 7. Look (owner's choice, Oct 2026: like his inDrive reference pack)
Dark: page `#121316`, panels `#1E1F24`, fields `#2A2C33`, text white, secondary `#A6ABB8`; lime `#B4EF2A` (our own shade) for the ONE main action of a screen, with near-black text `#0E0F12`; amber `#F59E0B` for money to collect and warnings ONLY; red `#F04438` for danger. Map-first screens, rounded panels (24) that follow the finger and rest at three heights: folded, normal (their content) and full screen (the content scrolls only at full height, so dragging never fights scrolling), large buttons, Feather line icons. While a finger is on the map or the map moves, its pins, floating buttons and the bottom panel disappear (only the map stays) and come back when it stops (the centre pin stays); detected with the map's own move events because the native map keeps the finger's moves to itself. A grey band `#26282E` (our own grey) at the top of every screen, half the phone's status-bar height (owner's wish). Font: Cairo (covers Arabic and Latin). Never rely on colour alone. All colours live in `mobile/src/lib/theme.ts` (the receiver page and staff console have their own). App name and logo: NOT chosen yet.
